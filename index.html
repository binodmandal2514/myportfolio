Sweat-Powered Bioelectrochemical Energy Cell

M.Tech Research Project · Status: Manuscript in preparation

Overview

Porous activated carbon was synthesized from arrowroot leaves through two-stage carbonization followed by KOH-assisted chemical activation. The resulting carbon was incorporated as an electrolyte-retaining medium in a Zn | activated carbon | Cu bioelectrochemical cell, using human sweat as the electrolyte. The prototype produced measurable electrical output directly from sweat, including lighting an LED.

Key Results

  • Open-circuit voltage of approximately 0.9 V and peak current of approximately 36.6 mA using human sweat as the electrolyte.
  • Maximum power output of approximately 32.94 mW.
  • BET surface area of 414.76 m² g⁻¹ with an average pore diameter of 2.624 nm — a well-developed micro-mesoporous structure.
  • Electrical output increased with electrolyte ionic strength: DI water < tap water < seawater < human sweat.

Materials & Method

Arrowroot leaves (Vallikunnam, Alappuzha, Kerala) were washed, dried, and carbonized in two stages (250 °C then 450 °C under argon), then chemically activated with 6M KOH at 650 °C. The carbon was characterized using XRD, Raman, FTIR, XPS, FESEM-EDS, and nitrogen adsorption-desorption (BET/BJH) analysis, then assembled between zinc and copper electrodes (2×2 cm) as the active layer.

Why It Matters

This demonstrates a low-cost, sustainable route for converting agricultural biomass waste into a functional material for self-powered wearable and biomedical electronics — using a biofluid that's always available: human sweat.
